# Logo 101

I'm going to be blunt. Most introductions to programming make you learn six ideas before you can print a single line. Logo makes you learn one. That's the whole reason I still teach with it, and it's why this tutorial exists.

Thirty short sections. Each one ends with five exercises, and each one compares Logo with **Python** and **Raku**, because I want you to see what Logo does *differently*, not just take my word that it's a good first language.

## Which Logo is this?

This is **UCBLogo**, also called Berkeley Logo, the version Brian Harvey wrote and used in his *Computer Science Logo Style* books. It's the Logo that shows a `?` prompt and says `square defined` when you teach it a new word. Other Logos (MSWLogo, FMSLogo, Turtle Academy, LibreLogo) are cousins. Most of what you learn here carries over, but a few details differ, and I'll flag the ones that matter.

## How the page works

- Every Logo snippet has a **Run** button. It runs a real Logo interpreter in your browser. Snippets earlier on the page are replayed silently first, so a procedure you met three snippets ago is still defined. Edit nothing, just press the button and read what comes out.
- Python and Raku snippets are for reading, not running. I want you to compare, not install things.
- At the end of each section there are **five exercises**. Three are multiple choice. One asks you to *type* what a piece of Logo prints; one asks you to *write* the Logo yourself. Your typed answers are checked by running them through the interpreter, not by matching against my answer, so any correct solution passes.
- Stuck twice? A "show me" button appears. Using it is fine, it just doesn't count as solved on your own.
